<objective>
Make stock actually move. A household member can take food off the shelf and have the count follow, and can call dibs on something without taking it yet.

Purpose: this is where the two actions CONTEXT.md spent the most care separating finally land. Consuming draws real units out of real batches, soonest-expiring first, and is refused when it would take more than the household physically has. Reserving marks a quantity with a name and refuses nothing — D-05 and D-07 are explicit that a reservation is a coordination signal, not a hold. Building them as one pipeline, or applying the overbooking guard to both, would quietly produce a different product than the one that was designed.

Output: FIFO consumption with a guard that never partially applies, independent reservations that anyone can see and only their author can release, three REST endpoints, and the controls for all of it on each item row.

Written test-first: the drain order, the guard boundary, and the ownership rule are exact input-to-output contracts with sharp edges, and every one of them is cheaper to pin down as a failing test than to debug later against live data.

**A note on `availability` that the implementer must not "fix".** Three numbers coexist deliberately:
- `capacity` — units physically on hand, the sum of the batch quantities. This is what the consume guard checks against (D-06: consume draws from total stock regardless of reservations).
- `reservedQuantity` — the sum of the reservations, informational.
- `availability` — `capacity` minus `reservedQuantity`, floored at zero, shown on screen as "units nobody has claimed" (INV-01).

So an item can show availability 0 and still accept a consume, because two of its three units are merely spoken for. That asymmetry is the whole point of D-05, and it is not a bug to be reconciled.
</objective>

Database-layer signatures, following the module's (client, ...) first-argument convention:
  consumeFromItem(client, householdId, location, rawName, quantity)      -> item dict
  addReservation(client, householdId, location, rawName, quantity, userId, userName) -> (reservationId, item dict)
  removeReservation(client, householdId, reservationId, userId)          -> item dict

Optimistic-concurrency contract for consumeFromItem:
  read the item, check quantity <= capacity, compute the drained batch list, then apply it with a
  single find_one_and_update whose filter pins _id AND the exact capacity AND the exact
  reservedQuantity that were read. A no-match means someone else wrote first: re-read and re-run the
  whole check, up to 3 attempts, then raise ConcurrentModificationError (409). Never write a
  partially drained batch list.

<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| browser → Flask REST API | Quantities, userIds, and reservationIds are all attacker-controlled and now drive mutations of shared household stock. |
| one household member → another member's reservation | Releasing someone else's claim is an authorization decision inside a household. |
| concurrent writers → one item document | Two members acting at the same moment race for the same stock. |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-02-03 | Tampering | quantity handling in `consumeFromItem` and `addReservation` | high | mitigate | Both functions validate quantity as an int of at least 1 and reject bools and numeric strings via `InvalidInventoryInput`, mapped to 400, before any read or write. Without this a negative consume would inflate stock and a negative reserve would inflate availability. Asserted by the consume 0, consume -1, non-integer, and reserve 0/-1 cases in Tasks 1 and 2. |
| T-02-08 | Elevation of privilege | `removeReservation` in server/hardwareDatabase.py | high | mitigate | The pull filter matches on `reservationId` AND `userId` together, so another member's identifier cannot remove a claim they did not create; a mismatch raises `ReservationNotOwnedError`, mapped to 403 with the entry intact. This implements D-08 and is asserted by the cross-member release test in Task 2. The client hiding the button is presentation only and is not the control. |
| T-02-09 | Tampering | concurrent consumes against one item | high | mitigate | The drained batch list is applied by a single `find_one_and_update` whose filter pins the exact `capacity` and `reservedQuantity` that were read; a losing writer matches nothing, re-reads, and re-runs the guard, exhausting after three attempts to `ConcurrentModificationError` mapped to 409. Because the new batch list is computed in full before the write, a rejected or lost consume leaves the stored item unchanged rather than partially drained. |
| T-02-14 | Spoofing | `userId` and `userName` supplied in the reserve and consume request bodies | medium | mitigate | Every one of the three routes calls `assertHouseholdMember` before acting, so an identifier outside the household is rejected with 403 (plan 02-01, T-02-01). Within a household the identifier is still self-asserted until Track A's session layer (ACCT-04) supplies it; that is Track A's boundary to close, and this plan's guard is positioned to read the authenticated identity without a signature change once it lands. |
| T-02-06 | Repudiation | consume actions are not attributed to a member | medium | accept | No stakeholder need or requirement in REQUIREMENTS.md calls for a consumption audit trail, and the PoC's trust model is a household of housemates who already share physical access to the food. Reservations do carry their author, so the one action with a social consequence is attributed. Recorded as a candidate for the v2 backlog rather than silently dropped. |

**ASVS level 1, block on high.** All three high-severity threats sit on the primary browser→API boundary and are mitigated with named functions and tested assertions. T-02-14 is mitigated to the extent Track B owns the boundary, with the remainder explicitly assigned to Track A's ACCT-04. T-02-06 is accepted with the rationale above.

**Package legitimacy:** this plan installs nothing; every package the phase uses was verified at plan 02-01 Task 1's `gate="blocking-human"` checkpoint.
